WORKING DRAFT

02 / THE READING ROOM

Supporting literature

A starting bibliography, organized by the questions in the matrix. These studies explain methods and limitations. They are not a comprehensive review or a ranking of available products.

01

Abatzoglou and Brown (2012) · International Journal of Climatology

A comparison of statistical downscaling methods suited for wildfire applications ↗

Describes and compares the MACA method for wildfire-relevant meteorology.

Method paper, published online in 2011 and in the 2012 journal volume. Archive coverage comes from the MACAv2 provider documentation.

DOI: 10.1002/joc.2312
02

Bukovsky and Mearns (2020) · Climatic Change

Regional climate change projections from NA-CORDEX and their relation to climate sensitivity ↗

Analyzes NA-CORDEX regional projections and their relation to parent-model climate sensitivity.

RCP8.5 experiments available at the time of the study, not every archive configuration or later release.

DOI: 10.1007/s10584-020-02835-x
03

Bhatia & Ganguly (2019) · Scientific Reports

Precipitation extremes and depth-duration-frequency under internal climate variability ↗

The study uses initial-condition ensembles to examine precipitation extremes and return levels. Combining member information can reduce sampling uncertainty under the study's distributional assumptions.

CESM large-ensemble analysis. Pooling members in another application requires checking the assumptions.

Annual maxima ↗
DOI: 10.1038/s41598-019-45673-3
04

Gebrechorkos et al. (2023) · Scientific Data

A high-resolution daily global dataset of statistically downscaled CMIP6 models for climate impact analyses ↗

Describes the global BCCAQ dataset, references, models, time periods and scenarios.

Dataset properties, not a universal regional skill ranking.

DOI: 10.1038/s41597-023-02528-x
05

Gergel et al. (2024) · Geoscientific Model Development

Global Downscaled Projections for Climate Impacts Research (GDPCIR): preserving quantile trends for modeling future climate impacts ↗

Describes QDM bias adjustment followed by QPLAD spatial downscaling for 25 CMIP6 models.

2024 dataset description. Preservation of modeled quantile changes is not evidence that those future changes are correct.

DOI: 10.5194/gmd-17-191-2024
06

Hartke et al. (2024) · Scientific Data

GARD-LENS: A downscaled large ensemble dataset for understanding future climate and its uncertainties ↗

Documents a 200-member downscaled ensemble from three CMIP6 models and comparisons with other products.

Historical and SSP3-7.0 ensemble. Three parent models do not represent the full range of model or scenario uncertainty.

DOI: 10.1038/s41597-024-04205-z
07

Hartke et al. (2025) · Environmental Research Letters

Lack of clear standards and usable comparisons of downscaled climate projections pose a roadblock for US climate discovery and adaptation ↗

Identifies gaps in comparable downscaling evaluations and argues for coordinated benchmarking.

Supports documenting evaluation context and uncertainty; not a universal ranking of products.

DOI: 10.1088/1748-9326/adc74e
08

Jennings et al. (2018) · Nature Communications

Spatial variation of the rain-snow temperature threshold across the Northern Hemisphere ↗

Observed rain-snow transition temperatures vary geographically. Including humidity improves phase prediction relative to temperature-only methods in the conditions examined.

Northern Hemisphere observational analysis. It does not prescribe a universal threshold or validate a specific downscaled product.

Rain versus snow ↗
DOI: 10.1038/s41467-018-03629-7
09

Jones et al. (2023) · Scientific Data

Continental United States climate projections based on thermodynamic modification of historical weather ↗

Documents WRF simulations that repeat historical weather with four thermodynamic warming trajectories.

Conditional warming experiments using a repeated 40-year weather sequence, not independently generated future weather realizations.

DOI: 10.1038/s41597-023-02485-5
010

Kendon et al. (2017) · Bulletin of the American Meteorological Society

Do Convection-Permitting Regional Climate Models Improve Projections of Future Precipitation Change? ↗

The reviewed convection-permitting simulations improve several subdaily rainfall characteristics. That finding does not imply uniform improvements in daily means, or make grid spacing alone a skill measure.

Regional convection-permitting modeling evidence. Applicability depends on location, process, and configuration.

Annual maxima ↗
DOI: 10.1175/BAMS-D-15-0004.1
011

Lange (2019) · Geoscientific Model Development

Trend-preserving bias adjustment and statistical downscaling with ISIMIP3BASD (v1.0) ↗

This method separates bias adjustment from spatial downscaling and explicitly handles dry-day frequency. Its evaluation illustrates why trend preservation and spatial variability need their own checks.

Evidence about ISIMIP3BASD v1.0. It does not establish the behavior of every bias-adjusted product.

Annual totals ↗Wet & dry spells ↗Storms across a region ↗
DOI: 10.5194/gmd-12-3055-2019
012

Lehner et al. (2020) · Earth System Dynamics

Partitioning climate projection uncertainty with multiple large ensembles and CMIP5/6 ↗

Multiple single-model ensembles help separate internal variability from differences in model response. The study shows why those uncertainty sources need separate treatment at regional scales.

Climate-model uncertainty analysis; not a downscaled product ranking.

Annual totals ↗Rain versus snow ↗
DOI: 10.5194/esd-11-491-2020
013

Maraun (2013) · Journal of Climate

Bias Correction, Quantile Mapping, and Downscaling: Revisiting the Inflation Issue ↗

Using quantile mapping to bridge mismatched spatial scales can misrepresent dependence and area-mean extremes. Matching local distributions is therefore insufficient evidence of credible regional precipitation.

A methodological caution about scale mismatch. This is not a version-specific evaluation of STAR or another named product.

Annual maxima ↗Storms across a region ↗
DOI: 10.1175/JCLI-D-12-00821.1
014

Pierce et al. (2014) · Journal of Hydrometeorology

Statistical Downscaling Using Localized Constructed Analogs (LOCA) ↗

Introduces localized constructed analog downscaling.

Original method; distinguish CMIP5 LOCA products, evaluation regridding, and later LOCA2 releases.

DOI: 10.1175/JHM-D-14-0082.1
015

Pierce et al. (2023) · Journal of Hydrometeorology

Future Increases in North American Extreme Precipitation in CMIP6 Downscaled with LOCA ↗

Describes LOCA2 downscaling and North American daily precipitation extremes from 27 CMIP6 models.

The 2023 study predates the precipitation update v20240915. Findings are not a validation of every application or later release.

DOI: 10.1175/JHM-D-22-0194.1
016

Rahimi et al. (2024) · Geoscientific Model Development

An overview of the Western United States Dynamically Downscaled Dataset (WUS-D3) ↗

Describes 16 WRF simulations driven by 14 CMIP6 models and evaluates western US climate.

Original 2024 ensemble with uncorrected GCM forcing; later bias-corrected experiments require separate evaluation.

DOI: 10.5194/gmd-17-2265-2024
017

Thrasher et al. (2013) · Eos, Transactions American Geophysical Union

Downscaled Climate Projections Suitable for Resource Management ↗

NEX-DCP30 publication cited by the Earth Engine distribution catalog.

Bibliographic metadata checked in the distributor citation. Full text was not retrieved in this catalog update; technical claims use the NASA documentation.

DOI: 10.1002/2013EO370002
018

Thrasher et al. (2012) · Hydrology and Earth System Sciences

Technical Note: Bias correcting climate model simulated daily temperature extremes with quantile mapping ↗

Describes quantile-mapping treatment of daily temperature extremes and is cited by the NEX-GDDP distributor.

Temperature methods paper; it does not establish precipitation skill or all metadata for the NEX-GDDP archive.

DOI: 10.5194/hess-16-3309-2012
019

Thrasher et al. (2022) · Scientific Data

NASA Global Daily Downscaled Projections, CMIP6 ↗

Describes the original NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 archive and its bias-correction/spatial-disaggregation method.

The 2022 archive description; later versions require their own release notes.

DOI: 10.1038/s41597-022-01393-4
020

Maraun et al. (2019) · International Journal of Climatology

Statistical downscaling skill under present climate conditions: A synthesis of the VALUE perfect predictor experiment ↗

VALUE evaluates multiple aspects of downscaling skill, including variability and dependence. Performance for one statistic does not establish skill for all application-relevant features.

Present-climate perfect-predictor evaluation. Future transfer and errors in driving climate models need additional assessment.
